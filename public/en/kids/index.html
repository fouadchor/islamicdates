Amazing facts about the Hijri calendar

🐪The Hijri calendar began with the Hijra of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ from Mecca to Medina more than 1400 years ago!
📅The Hijri year is about 11 days shorter than the Gregorian year, so Ramadan comes a little earlier each year.
👀A Hijri month only begins when the little crescent is sighted in the sky after sunset.
🌕Days 13, 14 and 15 of each month are called “the White Days” because the moon is a bright full moon then.

Teaching the Hijri Calendar to Children

This page is designed to help children understand the Hijri calendar in a fun, interactive way. A child learns how the moon phases change from the crescent to the full moon and then to the new moon during the lunar month, memorises the names and meanings of the twelve Hijri months — from Muharram to Dhul-Hijjah — through illustrated cards and the month-ordering game, practises spotting the crescent in the find-the-crescent game just as adults do on the 29th night of each month, then tests their knowledge in a short quiz, reads the illustrated story of the Prophet’s Hijra to learn why the calendar is called “Hijri”, and discovers their own Hijri birth date using the Umm al-Qura calendar. Parents and teachers can use these activities at home or in the classroom to bring the idea of the lunar year and Islamic occasions such as Ramadan and the two Eids closer to young minds.
